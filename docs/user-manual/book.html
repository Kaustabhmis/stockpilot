1Getting started

Sign in

  1. Open www.domebox.in and press Sign in.
  2. Type the email (or username) and password your company gave you.
  3. Press Sign in.

Forgot your password? Press Forgot password? next to the password box. We email you a link. It works once and only for one hour.

Your very first sign-in can take a few seconds longer than usual. That is normal.

Who can do what

Everyone has one of three roles. Your role is written under your name, top right.

DoerDoes their own tasks. Can ask their manager or a department head for help, but cannot give work to a colleague.
HOD(head of department) Gives work to people, checks and closes it, sees the team's scores.
AdminRuns the company's account. Sets up people, plans and settings.

A Doer only sees work that touches them: given to them, raised by them, waiting on them, or offered to them.

The tabs along the top

TabWhat it is for
TasksYour board — every task, in columns by where it stands.
PriorityOne list: what to do first, and why.
ProjectsBig jobs done in stages, one after another.
MeetingsThe weekly review meeting.
GoalsCompany purpose, values, goals and key numbers.
LeaderboardWho is on top this month.
ReportsScores and trends (team leads).
TeamThe people and who reports to whom.

The tabs run in the order you use them: your work first, then the weekly meeting and the company’s goals, then the leaderboard, then the tabs for team leads.

Next to the tabs: the bell (your notifications), the sparkle (ask questions about the numbers, where your plan includes it), the ? (help) and your name (your account).

Help is always one key away

Press the ? key, or the ? button in the top bar. Help opens on the page for the tab you are on. Type in the search box to find anything.

The User manual (PDF) button under the list downloads this book.

On your phone

Domebox works in the phone's browser — no app to install. The tabs move into a bar at the bottom. Help is in your account panel.

2Your task board

The Tasks tab is where you spend most of your day. It has two parts.

  • The boxes on top count your work: Pending, In progress, For review, Overdue — plus your Delegation score and Final score.
  • The columns below hold one card for each task.

The columns

ColumnMeans
Needs approvalSomeone asked for this work. A manager must say yes first.
To doAccepted. Not started yet.
In progressBeing worked on.
For reviewHanded in. Waiting for someone to check it.
VerifiedChecked and closed. Well done.

A red line on the left of a card means it is late or close to late. Small tags on a card show the due date, the priority, the category, a goal it serves, and REWORK 1 if it was sent back once.

Find a task fast

  • Search tasks… — type any word from the title.
  • Everyone — pick one person to see only their cards.
  • Any status / Any category / Any goal — narrow it down.
  • Overdue — tick to see only late work.
  • Board / List — cards in columns, or one table.

When a column gets long

Each column shows its 20 most urgent cards — late ones first, then the nearest due date. Press Show all at the bottom of the column to see the rest, and Show fewer to fold it again. The number at the top of a column always counts every card.

Old work: the Archive

A closed task leaves the board 7 days after it closed, so the board stays about live work. It is not lost. Press Archive to see closed work, newest first, 50 at a time. Press Load 50 more at the bottom for older ones. Search and filters work on the whole archive.

3Working on a task

Click any card. A panel opens on the right with everything about that task.

The life of a task

To do→ Start work →In progress→ Submit for review →For review→ checked →Verified

If the checker is not happy, they send it back. It returns to In progress with a new deadline and a note saying what to fix. That is called a rework loop.

The buttons

ButtonWhen to press it
Start workWhen you begin. The card moves to In progress.
Submit for reviewWhen it is done. Add a short note: what you did, where the proof is. Your on-time score is counted from this moment, not from when somebody gets round to checking it.
Hand overWhen someone else should do it. Pick the person and give a reason. Your manager decides — the task does not just vanish from your list.
Add blockerWhen you cannot start until another task is done. This task then says Blocked by… until that one closes.

Checklist

Some tasks come with a checklist. Tick each line as you finish it. The card shows how many are done, like 3/5. You can still hand in with lines unticked — it is a reminder, not a lock.

History

At the bottom of every task is its full history: every change, who made it, when, and their note. If you ever think a score is wrong, the history is your proof.

You cannot check and close your own work. If you raised a task for yourself, someone else signs it off.

4Giving work to someone

Press + Assign on the Tasks tab. Fill in the form.

BoxWhat to write
TaskWhat needs doing. Clear enough that anyone could check it is done.
DetailExtra facts, and what “done” looks like.
Assign toTick one person or several. Each person gets their own copy.
Due dateMust be filled. Scores depend on it.
PriorityCritical, High, Medium or Low. Important work counts for more.
Job categoryThe kind of work, e.g. Despatch, Quality.
Serves goalThe company goal this task helps (optional).
RepeatsFor work that happens again and again — see chapter 5.
ChecklistOne step per line (optional).

Before you send, the form tells you whether a manager must approve it first.

Where does the task go?

Who gave itWhat happens
Your own manager, or an AdminLands straight on your board.
You, for yourselfLands straight on your board.
A Doer asking their manager for helpThe manager accepts or declines it themselves.
Anyone elseGoes to Needs approval. The person's manager says yes or no.
Why can a Doer not give work to a colleague? Work dropped on you by a colleague, with no manager involved, is how people end up with eleven jobs and no way to say no. Ask your manager instead, or use Hand over on the task.

5Repeating tasks

For work that comes back on a schedule — a daily stock count, a weekly report, a monthly calibration.

  1. In the Assign form, open Repeats.
  2. Choose how often: Daily, Weekdays (Monday to Friday), Weekly, Fortnightly, Monthly, Quarterly, Half-Yearly or Yearly.
  3. Choose when it should stop.
Stop repeatingMeans
NeverKeeps going until someone stops it.
On a dateNo new copy after that date.
After a number of timesStops after that many copies.

Each new copy appears on time, even if the last one has not been checked yet. The card shows how far along it is, like Monthly · 3 of 6.

Monthly work set for the 31st lands on the last day of shorter months (28th or 30th) instead of slipping into the next month.

To end a series early, open any copy and press Stop repeating.

6Priority — what to do first

The Priority tab answers one question: what should I do next? It puts all your open work in one list, most pressing on top, and every line says why it is there.

What pushes a task up the list:

  • It is late — and it climbs a little more each day it stays late.
  • It is due today, tomorrow, or this week.
  • Other people are waiting on it — their task is blocked by yours.
  • You have already started it — finishing beats starting.
  • Its priority — a Critical task outranks a Low one with the same date.

Use the horizon buttons — Today, This week, This month, This quarter, This year, Everything — to look further ahead. Late work shows in every horizon, even Today.

Below the main list:

  • Waiting — blocked on someone else. Not your move yet.
  • Decisions — things waiting for you to approve or check.
  • Handed in — your work waiting to be checked.

7Projects

A project is a big job done in stages, one after another — an audit, a new-part approval, a machine install. Each stage has its own owner and its own deadline.

  • Every stage is a normal task on its owner's board.
  • Only stage 1 is live at the start. The others say Blocked.
  • When a stage is verified, the next stage starts by itself and its owner gets an email. Nobody has to remember to pass it on.
If the stage before yours finished late, your deadline moves by the same number of days. You get back the time that was planned for you. You are never marked late for someone else's delay.

To start one: Projects → New project. Give it a name, then add at least two stages — title, owner, deadline and priority for each.

8Your score

Your score is between 0 and 100. Click See why on the Final score box to see exactly how it was worked out, task by task. There are no hidden marks.

It is this month's score: the work you closed this month and what you are still holding. The leaderboard, Reports and your appraisal all use the same number. Until you close something in the month it says no data yet — that is not a zero.

Three things are measured

PartShareThe question it asks
On time45%Did you hand it in by the due date?
First-time right30%Was it accepted without being sent back?
Nothing left late25%Is any work you are still holding overdue?

On a project, a part of the score also comes from meeting each stage's deadline.

  • Each working day late takes 10 points off that task (weekends and approved leave are not counted). Ten working days late = zero for that task.
  • Each send-back (rework) takes 25 points off that task.
  • Critical tasks count for more than Low ones.

Doing more work counts

Someone who does one easy task on time should not beat someone who carries ten and delivers seven on time. So the score also looks at how much work you carried compared with what is normal for your job. Ten jobs with seven on time scores far higher than one easy job.

If you had very little work in a month, the score is marked provisional — too little to judge, not a bad mark.

These never count against you

  1. Approved leave and holidays. Late days do not add up while you are on leave your manager has recorded.
  2. A slow checker. You are timed until you press Submit for review — not until someone checks it.
  3. Work out of your hands. Tasks waiting for review or approval do not count as late on you.

Bands

BandScoreMeans
A85 and aboveTop performer
B60 to 84Solid, can get sharper
Cbelow 60Needs help
Think your score is wrong? Open See why, find the task, and look at its history. Then talk to your manager. A due date changed only by word of mouth is not in the system — ask for it to be changed in the task, with the reason.

9Leaderboard and cookie points

The leaderboard

The Leaderboard tab ranks the team from top to bottom for a week, month, quarter or year. It uses the same score as your dashboard. ▲ and ▼ show movement since last time.

  • Nobody is ranked on nothing. If you closed no work in the period (for example, you were on leave), you are listed under Not ranked with the reason — not at the bottom.
  • One easy task cannot win it, because workload counts.
  • People with the same score share a rank.

Your company decides who sees the whole table. You always see your own place.

Cookie points

Some good work does not show in a score: staying late to clear a backlog, catching a mistake nobody saw, helping a colleague finish. Managers can thank you for it with cookie points.

  • 1 to 5 points each time, always with a written reason.
  • You get an email when you receive them.
  • They can lift your score by 10 points at most, however many you get.

10Goals and key numbers

The Goals tab shows where the company is going and how it wants to get there.

  • Our purpose — one sentence: why the company exists.
  • Our values — how we behave. Each has a short code that people say out loud.
  • Year goals — what the company will achieve this financial year (April to March).
  • Quarter goals — the few things this quarter that move a year goal. Q1 is April to June.

Progress is counted, not guessed

The bar shows how much of the work linked to the goal has been verified — for example 6/9. To link your task to a goal, choose it under Serves goal when you assign the task.

Goal status

StatusMeans
On courseWill be done in time.
At riskMight not be. You must write one line saying why.
DoneFinished.
DroppedNo longer being chased.

If you own a goal, you can change its status yourself — the person closest to the work knows first when it is at risk.

Key numbers

Further down are the few weekly numbers that say if the week went well — despatches, rejections, collections. Each has an owner and a target. If you own one, enter this week's figure. A miss shows in red and should be raised as a roadblock in the weekly meeting.

11The weekly meeting

The Meetings tab runs your weekly review. One person — usually a manager — chairs it. Everyone else opens the same meeting on their own screen, and it follows the chair by itself.

The eight parts, always in this order

PartWhat happens
1 · WinsWho is here, and one good thing each — from work or home.
2 · Values in actionA short story of someone living one of the company values. It must name the value.
3 · Goal checkEach quarter goal: on course, at risk or done. No discussion here.
4 · Key numbersThis week's figures against their targets.
5 · UpdatesAnything everyone should know. One line each.
6 · RoadblocksProblems and chances, solved one at a time.
7 · ActionsLast week's actions — done or not? This week's: who, what, by when.
8 · CloseEveryone rates the meeting from 1 to 10.

A clock shows how long each part is taking. It turns red when a part runs over.

Share a win, a story or an update

Type in the box and press Share. Your point appears for everyone straight away.

Roadblocks

Raise a problem in part 6. It stays on the list from one week to the next until someone clears it. You can also raise one between meetings: Meetings → Raise a roadblock.

Turn any point into a task — Delegate

Every point in the meeting has a Delegate button. Press it, write what will be done, tick who (one person or several), and set the due date. Each person gets a real task on their board, with reminders, and it counts in their score. The point then shows who it went to and how far it has got, so nobody hands out the same job twice. Next week's meeting opens with every open action on the list.

Close — rate the meeting

Press a number from 1 to 10. If you give less than 8, say why. Ratings are anonymous: everyone sees how many have rated, nobody sees who gave what.

When the chair ends the meeting, everyone who was there gets a summary by email, with their own actions at the top.

Press Leave screen to go back to your other work; you can come back while the meeting is live.

12Bell and email reminders

The bell

The bell at the top shows a red number when something new needs you: a task given to you, a task sent back, a request for approval, cookie points, and so on. Click it to see the list; click a line to open that task.

Email reminders

Domebox emails you about your open tasks (To do or In progress) at 9 am, 3 pm and 5 pm, most urgent first:

  • on the day the task is given to you,
  • 2 days before it is due (the Friday before, if that falls on a weekend),
  • and every working day while it is late.

Hand the task in and the reminders stop. Nothing is sent about work waiting for review or approval, about closed work, or while you are on approved leave. On a day with nothing to say, nothing is sent at all.

If other people are waiting on your decision — an approval or a check — you also get one short email each morning listing them.

All emails come from info@biscsindia.com. If you cannot find them, look in Spam and mark them “Not spam”.

13For team leads

HODs and managers

As a team lead, your board also shows your team's work. Work waiting on you sits in Needs approval and For review.

Approve or refuse new work

A card in Needs approval is someone asking for your person's time. Open it and press Approve to put it on their board, or Reject with a reason.

Check handed-in work

Open a card in For review. Read the note and look at the proof.

  • Happy with it? Press Verify & close.
  • Not right? Press Send back. Say what to fix and give a new deadline.
You are timed too. Try to approve or check within 2 working days. After that, your own score loses a little each working day (never more than 20 points in total). Your team is never marked late for your slow check.

Give cookie points

Open the cookie panel, pick the person, give 1 to 5 points and write why. They get an email.

Reports

The Reports tab shows your team for a week, month, quarter or year: the team score, work delivered, the on-time share, rework loops and late work, the trend over twelve periods, how many people sit in each band, and each person's score.

Accountability shows rework by person and who is holding work up. Review history lists past appraisals.

Appraisal

Reports → Appraisal. Pick the person. Rate each responsibility area (KRA) and each behaviour from 0 to 5, add up to 5 bonus points if deserved, and write your remarks.

The final appraisal score is half your ratings and half the person's score from their task record. A new joiner with no closed work is judged only on your ratings — a new joiner is not a poor performer.

Reading a score fairly

  • Provisional means too little work to judge.
  • Good on-time work but a low “nothing left late” part usually means too much work, not slow work.
  • Many rework loops are usually about unclear briefing, not effort.
  • Open See why together before any performance talk.
Leave must be recorded in Domebox (Team → Leave) so late days stop counting for that person. Adding people, setting up categories and changing company settings are done by your Admin.

14Common questions

I cannot give a task to my colleague.

That is on purpose. Ask your manager to assign it, or raise it to them. If a task you hold should go to a colleague, use Hand over.

Why can I not close my own task?

A job nobody checked is not really closed. Someone else signs it off.

My task disappeared from the board.

Closed work leaves the board 7 days after it closed. Press Archive to find it.

My due date was changed, but it still says late.

Only a change made inside the task counts. Ask the person who gave it to change the due date in the task, with the reason.

I was on leave and it still says I was late.

Your leave must be recorded in Domebox. Ask your manager to add it.

I am not on the leaderboard.

You closed no work in that period, so you are listed under Not ranked. That is not last place.

A task says “Blocked”. What do I do?

Open it to see which task it is waiting on. You can start when that one is verified. If it is stuck, raise it with your manager or as a roadblock in the weekly meeting.

I am not getting any emails.

Look in Spam for mail from info@biscsindia.com. Still nothing? Tell your Admin.

I forgot my password.

Press Forgot password? on the sign-in screen. The email link works once, for one hour.

Still stuck? Press ? and choose Contact support at the foot of any help page, or write to info@biscsindia.com. We reply within one working day.
